# Research question matrix

[Research index](README.md) · [Methods](STUDY_DESIGN_COMPARISON.md)

All rows are proposed. Each question specifies the target population, exposure or predictor, outcome and core covariates. The methods column states the design, major confounding/selection risks and limits.

| Dataset | Population, exposure or predictor, outcome and covariates | Design, method and principal bias |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [NHANES 2011–2014](datasets/nhanes.md) | Among adults aged 60+ with eligible cognitive assessments, is lower poverty-income ratio associated with lower DSST performance after accounting for age, education, sex, race/ethnicity and survey cycle? | Pooled design-weighted cross-sectional regression with strata/PSU and eligible examination weights. Check functional form and compare complete-case estimates with a missingness sensitivity analysis. Educational opportunity and health may confound the association; survival and exam participation may select the sample. Temporal direction is unidentified. |
| [VCAP V1.0](datasets/vcap.md) | Among baseline-eligible VCAP adults, is education associated with Digit Symbol slope after adjusting for baseline age, age-by-time and recorded sex? | Use actual elapsed years in a mixed-effects model with education-by-time and participant random intercept/slope. Compare first-retest adjustment and repeat-only fits. Cohort effects, unmeasured socioeconomic differences and selective return limit interpretation; education is not randomized. |
| [OASIS-2](datasets/oasis-2.md) | Among participants with repeat scans, does baseline CDR group modify the annual nWBV trajectory after adjustment for baseline age, sex and education? | Use scan elapsed time and group-by-time in a parsimonious mixed model. Check robustness to restricting clinical-to-MRI timing and to excluding sparse CDR strata. Age, disease severity, and return/imaging selection may confound comparisons. Avoid conditioning on post-baseline CDR when defining baseline groups. |
| [OASIS-3](datasets/oasis-3.md) | Among participants with baseline T1-derived hippocampal volume and later clinical assessments, is smaller baseline volume associated with faster worsening in scored global CDR? | Define a clinical timing window anchored to baseline MRI; fit an ordinal mixed model with volume-by-time, age, sex, eTIV or equivalent verified head-size measure, and scanner/pipeline indicators. Repeat with a narrower window. Selection into imaging and clinical follow-up, diagnostic spectrum and scanner changes can bias estimates. |
| [MIDUS](datasets/midus.md) | Among original national-core participants with BTACT follow-up, is education associated with change in immediate recall between MIDUS 2 and 3 after accounting for baseline age and sex? | Model recall on elapsed time and education-by-time, cluster by respondent and family where applicable, and compare survey-design estimates with attrition-weighted sensitivity. Control age, sex and available early-life socioeconomic indicators once mapped. Initial education selection and differential telephone participation limit interpretation. |
| [NHATS](datasets/nhats.md) | Among baseline self-interviewed beneficiaries, does living alone predict poorer later immediate recall after age, sex, education, baseline recall and baseline limitations are accounted for? | Use a defined inception cohort and compatible longitudinal weight; estimate among survivors with follow-up, contrasting inverse-probability observation weighting and a proxy/competing mortality sensitivity. Health and support may confound; proxy respondents cannot be coded as low test scores. |
| [ADNI](datasets/adni.md) | Among a single ADNI phase's participants with baseline MCI, does baseline T1 hippocampal volume improve prediction of 24-month ADAS13 beyond baseline ADAS13, age, sex, education and site? | Subject-disjoint validation, site-held-out sensitivity, and preprocessing in training only. Define outcome window, harmonize one approved derivative, adjust for head size, benchmark MAE and calibrate uncertainty. Missing scans and delayed visits may select healthier people; referral enrichment limits transport. |
| [ELSA](datasets/elsa.md) | Among core ELSA members aged 50+ at a chosen entry wave, are lower baseline wealth and time associated with steeper decline in delayed word recall, controlling age, sex, education and baseline health? | Select common wave instrument, inflation-adjust the financial measure, fit wave/elapsed-time by wealth with random participant effects and assess design/attrition weights. Early-life disadvantage confounds and cognition may affect wealth; restrict a lagged analysis and report differential dropout. |
| [UAS CogCF](datasets/uas-cogcf.md) | Among adult panelists with comparable later-series word recall, does baseline education predict change in immediate recall across dated waves after adjusting for age, sex and baseline survey engagement? | Fit weighted mixed models with time-by-education and wave/mode indicators; assess respondent nonresponse and practice sensitivity using repeat-exposure count where verified. Test mode and engagement may be associated with education; repeat participants may not represent the whole panel. |
| [HRS / RAND HRS](datasets/hrs.md) | Among self-interviewed age-50+ HRS respondents in comparable waves, is baseline household wealth associated with later total word recall after age, sex, education and baseline self-reported health adjustment? | Use elapsed follow-up and wealth-by-time with survey-aware estimates, comparing observed and documented imputed cognitive measures and handling proxy and death separately. Prior cognition may affect wealth, and attrition may preferentially remove poorer or less healthy respondents. |
| [NACC UDS](datasets/nacc.md) | Among UDS participants with baseline MCI and later visits, is education associated with time to first recorded worsening in global CDR, conditional on age, sex, center, baseline CDR and protocol version? | Model interval-censored progression, treating death as competing where ascertainment permits. Stratify or adjust by center and test robustness to version and follow-up rules. Referral and differential return select cases; education may influence scoring and diagnosis. |
