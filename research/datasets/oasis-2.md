## Verified Dataset Characteristics

**Review status** — Metadata Only. Public sources reviewed 2026-11-24.

### Overview, population and structure

Marcus et al. report 150 Washington University older adults, ages 60–96, and 373 longitudinal imaging sessions. Multiple T1 acquisitions per session are distinct from repeat visits; clinical assessments and scans may be offset.

### Cognitive, social, biological and imaging measures

Primary documentation describes clinical CDR and MMSE, age, sex, education and socioeconomic status, and derived normalized whole-brain volume (nWBV), estimated total intracranial volume (eTIV) and atlas scaling factor. Assay availability: Not Verified.

### Strengths, limitations and added value

Same-scanner design and precomputed derivatives support a narrow pilot. Small clinical subgroups, selective repeat imaging and nonconcurrent assessments constrain the design. Adds structural MRI to VCAP and repeat MRI beyond NHANES.

### Access and governance

Access Restricted for implementation. WashU lists an application, institutional email and data-use terms. Terms require attribution and prohibit identification; AI permission Not Explicitly Specified in reviewed public materials.

## Proposed Research Design

**Testable question.** Among participants with repeat scans, does baseline CDR group modify the annual nWBV trajectory after adjustment for baseline age, sex and education?

**Design, analysis and limitations.** Use scan elapsed time and group-by-time in a parsimonious mixed model. Check robustness to restricting clinical-to-MRI timing and to excluding sparse CDR strata. Age, disease severity, and return/imaging selection may confound comparisons. Avoid conditioning on post-baseline CDR when defining baseline groups.

**Social and biological suitability.** Highest-feasibility imaging pilot if authorized derivatives and clinical timing fields are available; limited social depth.

## Completed Empirical Research

This profile is a public-source evaluation. The proposed design has no project sample estimate or model result.
